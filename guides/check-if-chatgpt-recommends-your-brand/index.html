How can I check whether ChatGPT recommends my brand or my competitors?

You can't manage what you haven't measured. When a buyer asks ChatGPT for the best tool in your category, the answer names a short list of brands. Either yours is on that list or your competitors are. Here is a straightforward way to find out — first by hand, then with a tool — and how to read what you find without fooling yourself.

— One buyer question, three engines

“What's the best [category] for [a specific use case]?”

PerplexityMentioned
GeminiNot in the answer

Illustrative record card — the same question, word for word, in each engine. Not a CitMark scan, and no score.

01

Write the questions your buyers actually ask

Don't start with your brand name. A question like "tell me about [your brand]" tells you nothing about visibility, because the answer will obviously discuss you. Start with buyer-intent questions — the kind a customer asks before they know your name:

  • "What's the best [category] for [a specific use case]?"
  • "[Option A] vs [Option B] for [a specific job] — which should I pick?"
  • "I'm a [type of team] with [a specific constraint]. What [category] should I use?"

Write down five to ten of these. Use the wording your customers use, not your marketing copy. These questions are your test set, and keeping them consistent is what makes any later comparison meaningful.

02

Ask them properly

Open a fresh conversation for each question. AI assistants remember context within a chat, and earlier messages can steer later answers — a fresh chat keeps each answer independent. Ask the question exactly as written; don't add hints like "please include…" or name your brand in a follow-up before the first answer is complete. If you want to test more than one engine, ask the same question, word for word, in each one. Answers differ between engines, and that difference is itself useful information.

03

Record answers in a simple table

For every answer, note four things:

  1. Mentioned? Does your brand appear in the answer — yes or no?
  2. Who else is named? List every competitor that appears. This is your real shortlist competition.
  3. In what context? A passing mention is weaker than being the recommended pick, and both are weaker than being recommended with reasons that match your strengths.
  4. Cited? Did the answer link to or reference sources — and is your site among them? A mention and a citation are different things (more on this below).

A spreadsheet with one row per question per engine is enough. What matters is that you record the answer as given, not as you wish it were.

How to read the results honestly

01

A mention is not a citation. A mention means the engine named your brand in its answer text. A citation means the engine pointed to a source — a page it drew on. You can be mentioned without being cited (the engine knows your name but sends readers elsewhere), and that gap tells you where your content is losing to someone else's.

02

Small samples carry wide uncertainty. Three questions across three engines is nine answers. If your brand appears in three of them, that's a signal, not a precise 33%. Treat a single snapshot as a directional reading with a wide confidence interval, not as an exact score. The honest response to a small sample is a bigger or repeated sample — not a celebration or a panic.

03

One run is a photograph, not a verdict. AI answers vary from run to run. A brand missing today may appear tomorrow with no action from anyone. That's why the value of a check like this is the pattern across questions and engines — consistently absent everywhere is a finding; absent once is noise.

04

Being absent is information, not a death sentence. If competitors are named and you are not, the answers themselves show you why: the reasons and sources the engine gives for its picks are a map of what it currently trusts in your category. That map is where improvement starts.

No tool — including CitMark — can guarantee that AI assistants will recommend your brand. What a careful check gives you is the truth about where you stand today, measured rather than guessed.
