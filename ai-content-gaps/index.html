AI Content Gaps: Find the Questions Where AI Names Competitors but Not You

An AI content gap is a buying question where an AI answer mentions one or more competitors but does not mention your brand. The gap is not simply a low visibility score. It is a specific, inspectable pattern: for this question, in this engine, these brands were named — and yours was not.

Run a free gap check

That pattern is valuable because it tells a content or marketing team exactly where to look next. A single aggregate number can say that visibility is weak. A gap list says which category questions, use-case questions, and comparison questions are leaving you out of the answer a buyer reads.

How to find AI content gaps

01

Build a prompt set around real buying questions

Start with the questions a buyer would ask before choosing a product in your category: best-tool questions, alternatives questions, use-case questions, and questions that name the job the buyer is trying to get done. Keep the set focused. A gap is only useful if the question represents demand you actually want to win.

02

Run the same questions in real AI answers

Check the questions in the engines your buyers use. CitMark's Starter and Growth plans watch ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini; Pro adds DeepSeek (synthetic). Its working principle is measured not modeled: it inspects real answers rather than estimating what an answer probably says. Manual spot checks can reveal an individual gap, but they are hard to repeat consistently across a full prompt set.

03

Compare mentions prompt by prompt

For each question, list the brands the answer mentioned and mark whether your brand was included. Four patterns appear:

Competitor-only

Competitors are mentioned and you are not. This is the content gap.

Shared

You and competitors are both mentioned. You are in the answer, but positioning may still be worth reviewing.

Yours only

You are mentioned and competitors are not. Protect whatever is working.

Neither

No relevant brand is named. The question may be poorly phrased, too early in the journey, or answered in a different format.

The competitor-only rows are the priority list. They are concrete: a named question, a named engine, and a named set of competitors who appeared instead of you.

Treat it as the diagnostic starting point — the list of questions worth investigating first.

What to do after you find a gap

Start with the competitor-only questions that are closest to purchase intent and most relevant to your product. For each one, read the answer and the pages it draws on, then compare them with your own content. Common fixes are at the method level: publish or strengthen a page that answers the question directly; make your category, use cases, and differentiators explicit rather than implied; add a clear comparison or alternatives page where buyers are actively weighing options; and pursue relevant third-party coverage or mentions through outreach where your product genuinely belongs in the list.

After making a change, rerun the same prompt set. Keeping the questions stable is what turns a one-off observation into a measurement: you can see whether the same question still produces a competitor-only answer, or whether your brand has started to appear. Results will vary by engine and by question, and no tool can honestly promise a recommendation. The value of the process is that each round replaces guessing with observed answers.
